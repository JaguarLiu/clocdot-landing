Compliance

What Taiwan expects from attendance records

This is the part that surprises most overseas HR teams: attendance obligations in Taiwan do not go away when the work happens off-site. Remote staff, employees working from home and field staff all need records — and "we track output, not hours" is not a substitute.

Three things to get right

  • Daily records, down to the minute. Not "present today" — the actual start and end of each working day needs to be reconstructable. This is why a Slack message saying "starting now" does not really count: it lives in a conversation thread, not in a record anyone can review.
  • Retained for a number of years. Records are not something you clear once payroll closes for the month. Paper cards and scattered chat logs are the riskiest option here — you often only discover whether they still exist at the moment someone asks for them.
  • Employees can request their own records. When an employee asks to see or copy their own attendance record, the employer is expected to provide it. So the system needs to show employees their own history, not just an HR back office.

Translated into a buying checklist, it comes down to three questions: is the record granular enough, does it survive long enough, and can the employee retrieve it? Distributed teams need to be especially careful — the more spread out people are, the less any physical device is quietly creating a record for you.

Yes. Record-keeping obligations do not disappear because the work happens off-site — employees working from home, remotely or in the field still need records, logged daily and to the minute, and retained for a number of years.
